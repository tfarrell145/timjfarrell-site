Broomstick Superpower

Word of the day: pulley

“Can you pull two grown-ups across the room all by yourself? Let's find out.”

Grab

  • two broom handles or mop handles
  • a rope about 4 m long
  • a second grown-up (or tie one broom to a heavy table leg)

Grown-ups hold on gently and let go if hands get pinched between handles.

Do

  1. Two grown-ups each hold a broom handle, about a meter apart, and try to keep them apart.
  2. Tie the rope to one handle and pull it straight. Hard to move.
  3. Now wrap the rope back and forth around both handles 4 or 5 times. Give the kid the free end.
  4. They pull. The grown-ups slide together. Try with fewer wraps and more wraps.

The big moment

A four-year-old drags two grown-ups across the room.

Ask (guess first, then try)

  • Did more wraps make it easier or harder?
  • Who is pulling farther, you or the brooms?

What's going on (for you)

Each wrap of rope adds another strand pulling the brooms together, like a block-and-tackle pulley. Five wraps can multiply their pull several times (friction eats some of it). The price: they have to pull a lot of rope to move the brooms a little. Same trade as the lever.

If they want more

Hang a rope over a banister or a broom laid between two chairs and pull down to lift a bucket of toys up. A single pulley lets you pull down to lift up. The bucket weighs the same.
