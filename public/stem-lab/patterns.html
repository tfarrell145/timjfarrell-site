Pattern Snakes

Word of the day: pattern

“I'm going to make a snake, and it has a secret code. Can you crack it?”

Grab

  • two kinds of pasta, or two colors of blocks, LEGO or crayons
  • a string (optional, for threading pasta)

Do

  1. Lay out red, blue, red, blue, red... and stop. Ask what comes next.
  2. Make an ABB pattern: red, blue, blue, red, blue, blue. Ask again.
  3. Let them make one and you guess. Get it wrong on purpose once so they get to correct you.
  4. Clap it: clap, stomp, clap, stomp. Patterns can be sounds too.

The big moment

You get one wrong on purpose and they catch you.

Ask (guess first, then try)

  • What comes next?
  • How did you know?
  • Can you make one I can't figure out?

What's going on (for you)

Spotting and extending patterns is the root of algebra: the rule matters more than the objects. Turning a color pattern into a clap pattern shows them that the rule is separate from the stuff.

If they want more

Look for patterns on clothes, tiles, fences, the days of the week.
