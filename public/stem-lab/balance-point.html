Balancing Act

Word of the day: balance

“Can you balance a ruler on one finger? What if I make one end heavy?”

Grab

  • a ruler or paint stirrer
  • a lump of play-dough
  • a pencil or marker

Do

  1. Balance the ruler on their finger. Where does it balance? (The middle.)
  2. Stick play-dough on one end. Try balancing it in the middle now. It falls.
  3. Slide the finger along until it balances again. Which way did the balance spot move?
  4. Mark the spot with the pencil.

The big moment

Add the dough and the ruler drops off their finger.

Ask (guess first, then try)

  • Why did it fall when we added the dough?
  • Which way did the balance spot move: toward the heavy end or away?

What's going on (for you)

Every object has a balance point, its center of gravity, where its weight is even on both sides. Adding weight to one end pulls the balance point toward that end. It's the lever again: heavy and close balances light and far.

If they want more

Tightrope walkers carry a long pole to keep their balance point low and steady. Walk a line of tape on the floor with arms out vs arms in.
